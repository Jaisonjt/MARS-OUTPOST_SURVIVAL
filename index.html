A NASA Space Apps Challenge project

MARS: OUTPOST SURVIVAL

Your mission. Your decisions. Their survival.

You are the Mars Outpost Commander. For 10 days you will lead a crew of 4 on the red planet.

Air, power, food, water and shielding are limited. Every choice has a cost, so choose wisely. Can you keep your crew safe?

A fictional educational simulation. Resource numbers are simplified gameplay assumptions, not operational NASA mission specifications.

How to Play

Your mission

You lead a small crew on a Mars outpost for 10 days. Keep everyone safe, keep the outpost running, and complete science objectives along the way.

Your five resources

    You also watch your crew health 🧑‍🚀 and your research 🔬 progress.

    Every day

    1. Check how your outpost is doing and note the daily resource consumption.
    2. Read the daily mission event: a dust storm, a clogged filter, or a space-weather alert.
    3. Hover over or focus on each choice to preview its exact resource trade-offs on the bars.
    4. Click your chosen action and read the real NASA science lesson behind the outcome.
    5. Advance to the next mission day.

    Warning signs

    Below 20 a resource shows ⚠️ Low. At zero it shows ⛔ Empty and reduces crew health each day.

    Winning and ending early

    You succeed if your crew survives all 10 days and you finish at least two of the three mission objectives: reach 40 Research points, harvest food in the greenhouse, and transmit a telemetry report to Earth.

    The mission ends early if crew health runs out or oxygen stays empty for two days in a row. Don't worry: the crew evacuates safely to orbit, and your debrief report will explain what to try next time!

    Science & Data Attribution

    Scientific Integrity: Four Kinds of Information

    To keep the science honest, this project separates real data from gameplay mechanics:

    • 1. Real NASA Data: Observed solar flare classifications (DONKI), space-biology plant studies (OSDR), MOXIE oxygen experiments, and Martian environmental facts.
    • 2. Derived Calculations: One-way light-time communication delays (ranging from 8 to 21 minutes across the mission) modeled from Earth–Mars orbital distance geometry.
    • 3. Simplified Game Assumptions: Resource meter scales (0–100) and daily consumption rates (-6 Oxygen, -6 Food, -6 Water, +4 Solar Energy) tuned for a 10-day educational session.
    • 4. Fictional Story Events: The specific sequence of outpost maintenance challenges experienced by your pretend crew.

    Authoritative NASA Sources Used

    1. NASA CCMC DONKI (Space Weather Database)

    Contribution: Inspires Day 4's solar flare & CME radiation alert. Teaches that a solar flare observation does not directly equal a fixed Mars surface radiation dose—actual exposure depends on shielding thickness, atmospheric depth, and event geometry. (Accessed: Nov 2026)

    2. NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR)

    Contribution: Grounds Day 3's hydroponic greenhouse harvest in real space-biology experiments (such as Veggie and Advanced Plant Habitat), showing how LED lighting and water allocation support crop growth in space without claiming a single study predicts exact crew health. (Accessed: Nov 2026)

    3. NASA Mars Exploration Program (MOXIE, MEDA & Surface Operations)

    Contribution: Informs Day 1 & 8 dust storm solar attenuation, Day 2 MOXIE oxygen generation from Martian CO₂, Day 6 Deep Space Network light-time delay, and Day 9 nighttime thermal swings (-90°C). (Accessed: Nov 2026)

    4. NASA Open APIs Gateway

    Contribution: Provides live space-weather verification via the DONKI FLR endpoint with graceful offline fallback. (Accessed: Nov 2026)
